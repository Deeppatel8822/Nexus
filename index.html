About Indian Cumin Seeds

India produces over 70% of the world's cumin supply. Indian cumin — known as jeera — is prized for its bold, warm aroma and high volatile oil content, making it the preferred choice for food processors, spice blenders, and packaged food manufacturers worldwide.

Every lot is machine-cleaned, graded, and tested by NABL-accredited labs for pesticide residue and aflatoxin before export. We offer FAQ, Singapore, and Europe grades to suit buyer requirements across all markets.

About Indian Green Cardamom

Green cardamom — "elaichi" — is often called the Queen of Spices, and Indian cardamom is the global benchmark for quality. While Guatemala produces greater volume, Kerala's Idukki district (over 80% of India's cardamom output) yields pods with significantly higher volatile oil content — typically 6.6% to 10.6% — giving Indian cardamom a more complex, camphor-like aroma that commands a price premium worldwide.

Nexus Global Exim sources cardamom through the Spices Board's regulated Kerala auction system, where pods are graded, cured, and prepared under close quality supervision. Our cardamom carries the "Alleppey Green" designation — a name protected by a Geographical Indication (GI) tag certifying genuine Kerala origin.

Every export lot ships with a Certificate of Analysis covering moisture, volatile oil content, and EU pesticide MRL compliance — an important detail since Indian cardamom currently sits on the EU's enhanced border monitoring list. We supply whole pods, split cardamom, and cardamom powder in moisture-proof, food-grade packaging built to protect colour and aroma through transit.

About Indian Black Pepper

Black pepper — often called the "King of Spices" — is the dried, unripe fruit (peppercorn) of the Piper nigrum vine. India's Malabar Coast in Kerala is one of the spice's original homelands, and pepper trade from this region drove much of early global maritime exploration, including Vasco da Gama's voyage to India.

Nexus Global Exim sources black pepper from the Malabar region of Kerala, including the Wayanad growing belt. Our standard export grade is Malabar Garbled (MG1) — the industry workhorse known for consistent quality and sharp, biting heat, suited to large-scale industrial use, spice blending, and foodservice. For buyers seeking a premium presentation grade, we supply Tellicherry pepper (TGEB/TGSEB) — large berries left to fully ripen on the vine before harvest, prized for their bolder, more complex, slightly citrus-tinged flavour.

Every export lot is tested at NABL-accredited labs for piperine content, moisture, and microbial levels, with increasing use of steam sterilisation to meet EU and US microbiological requirements. Bulk density — measured in grams per litre — is the key trade indicator of quality: a higher density (550 g/l or above) signals fully matured, well-developed peppercorns.

About Indian Turmeric

Turmeric — known as haldi in India and Curcuma longa botanically — has been cultivated and traded from India for over 4,000 years, once called "Indian saffron" for its vivid colour. India remains the world's largest producer, consumer, and exporter of turmeric, accounting for over 75% of global production. In January 2025, the Government of India established the National Turmeric Board, targeting USD 1 billion in turmeric exports by 2030 — a clear signal of the category's growing strategic importance.

Nexus Global Exim sources turmeric from India's most recognised growing belts — Erode in Tamil Nadu (GI-tagged in 2019, celebrated as the "Turmeric City of India") and Sangli in Maharashtra (GI-tagged in 2018, one of the country's largest turmeric trading hubs). We also supply high-curcumin varieties such as Nizamabad (Telangana) and Lakadong (Meghalaya) for buyers in the nutraceutical and pharmaceutical space who need curcumin content above standard commercial grades.

Every export lot is tested by HPLC (High-Performance Liquid Chromatography) — the accepted industry method for verifying exact curcumin percentage — along with moisture, ash content, and microbial safety. We also test to confirm the product is free from illegal adulterants such as lead chromate, a toxic dye occasionally used to enhance colour, which remains a serious quality and safety concern in the global turmeric trade.

About Indian Red Chilli

Red chilli (lal mirch), the dried fruit of Capsicum annuum, is India's largest spice export by value — the country accounts for over 36% of global chilli production and trade. Unlike most spices, red chilli quality is defined by two independent, lab-verified metrics: ASTA colour value (red pigment intensity) and SHU — Scoville Heat Units (pungency from capsaicin). These two numbers, not appearance alone, determine which buyers a given lot suits.

Nexus Global Exim supplies three benchmark varieties. Guntur Sannam (Andhra Pradesh) is the industry workhorse — balanced heat (30,000–35,000 SHU) and moderate colour (100–120 ASTA), used across curry powders, sauces, and general spice blends. Byadgi (Karnataka) trades heat for colour — mild pungency (8,000–16,000 SHU) paired with exceptional deep-red ASTA values (100–150+), making it the preferred raw material for oleoresin extraction, natural food colouring, and cosmetics. Teja (Telangana/Andhra Pradesh) sits at the opposite extreme — 75,000–110,000+ SHU — supplying the sauce, capsaicin-extraction, and extreme-heat segments.

Every export lot is tested for ASTA and SHU by accredited labs, along with aflatoxin, moisture, and microbial safety (Salmonella, E. coli). We supply whole pods (stem or stemless), crushed flakes, and powder, matched to your target ASTA/SHU specification.

About Indian Cloves

Cloves are the dried, unopened flower buds of Syzygium aromaticum, an evergreen tree harvested just before the bud opens, when it turns from green to a deep crimson, then dried to a rich reddish-brown. India is not among the world's largest producers by volume — that position belongs firmly to Indonesia — but Indian cloves, grown in the Idukki district of Kerala and the Kanyakumari region of Tamil Nadu, are valued for a quality difference buyers should know about: they run smaller than Indonesian cloves but carry meaningfully higher eugenol content, typically 70–90% versus lower concentrations in bulk Indonesian stock.

Eugenol is the aromatic compound responsible for clove's numbing, warming character, and it's the reason cloves are as important to toothpaste manufacturers, essential oil distillers, and pharmaceutical buyers as they are to spice traders. Higher eugenol content directly drives value for these industrial buyers, distinct from the whole-bud appearance that matters more to culinary buyers.

Quality grading in the clove trade centres on bud integrity. Premium Grade A / Hand-Picked Selected (HPS) stock is sorted to exclude "headless cloves" (buds where the aromatic ball has fallen off), "mother cloves" (over-matured, low-flavour fruit), "khoker" (withered or fermented buds), and "exhausted cloves" (buds already used for oil extraction, with little aroma left) — all of which compromise both flavour and eugenol yield.
